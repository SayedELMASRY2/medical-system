# ADR-002: Deterministic Risk Separation

## Status: Accepted
## Date: 2026-08-17

## Context
In AI-driven medical systems, determining patient risk levels is a critical function. Large Language Models (LLMs) are powerful at summarizing, reasoning, and explaining complex scenarios. However, they are prone to hallucinations, can be inconsistent across identical inputs, and lack strict reproducibility. Medical risk assessment, particularly when determining whether to escalate a case (e.g., triggering a 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY alert), requires deterministic, auditable, and reproducible logic based on established clinical guidelines.

## Decision
We will separate the calculation of the risk level from the LLM reasoning process. 
- **Risk Level Determination:** Controlled strictly by a deterministic Rule Engine. This engine evaluates patient data against hardcoded, medically sourced thresholds (e.g., Systolic BP > 160).
- **Explanation:** An LLM (Triage Agent) is used to explain the *result* of the deterministic engine, synthesize the clinical picture, and retrieve relevant evidence. The LLM cannot override or modify the risk level generated by the Rule Engine.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- **Reproducibility:** Risk assessments are 100% consistent and reproducible given the same input data.
- **Auditability:** The system provides a clear, auditable rule chain showing exactly why a risk level was assigned.
- **Safety:** Mitigates the risk of LLM hallucinations causing missed critical alerts or false positives.
- **Value Add:** The LLM still provides significant value by generating readable explanations and pulling in context, without bearing the safety burden of the final risk calculation.

**Negative:**
- **Maintenance:** The Rule Engine requires manual updates and maintenance as clinical guidelines change.
- **Rigidity:** The system cannot dynamically infer risk outside of the programmed rules, potentially missing nuanced cases that an expert (or an advanced LLM) might catch.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Pure LLM Risk Assessment:** Using the LLM to determine the risk level. Rejected due to unacceptable safety risks, lack of auditability, and inconsistency.
- **LLM as Primary with Rule Fallback:** Allowing the LLM to determine risk, but validating it against rules. Rejected as overly complex and still prone to edge cases where the LLM might hallucinate a rationale that bypassing safety checks.
